\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Legolization by optimisation.} Turning a shape into a brick layout has been posed as a layout
optimisation problem for over a decade: graph-connectivity heuristics that find and repair weak points
\citep{testuz2013constructable}, force-based stability with layout refinement \citep{luo2015legolization},
maximum-flow stability bounds \citep{wassmann2012maxflow}, genetic algorithms over brick layouts
\citep{lee2015galayout,lee2018smga}, automatic assembly manuals \citep{ono2013legobuilder}, and extensions to
Technic structures \citep{xu2019technic}. Our greedy search inherits this line's objectives (cover the target,
interlock layers, use few pieces) in a deliberately simple form. It is a frozen baseline and a teacher, not a
contribution.

\paragraph{Learned brick generation.} Sequential assembly was first learned with Bayesian optimisation
\citep{kim2020combinatorial} and then with reinforcement learning: Brick-by-Brick \citep{chung2021brickbybrick}
trains a PPO policy with a learned validity mask and a per-step IoU reward, ending each episode when a per-task
brick budget is spent, with no stop action. BrECS \citep{ahn2024brecs} replaces RL with a 3D-convolutional
placement scorer behind a constraint filter, trained from a procedural teacher under explicit per-type brick
budgets, and reports that this beats the RL and generative baselines; it is the closest relative of our SFT arm.
Image- and text-conditioned generators have since adopted a stability oracle: BrickGPT \citep{pun2025brickgpt}
uses StableLego for rejection sampling and rollback, a rollback-free successor trains with physics rewards after
SFT and reports that the RL stage also cuts brick counts \citep{xu2026stable}, and BrickAnything
\citep{ni2026brickanything}, BrickNet \citep{kulits2026bricknet}, LEGO-Maker \citep{ge2025legomaker},
Image2Lego \citep{lennon2021image2lego} and ResemBrick \citep{chen2026resembrick} cover geometry, graph, image and
photograph inputs. In the works we could verify, the budgeted learners carry no physics and the physics-aware
generators carry no budget; we found none that combines an explicit piece budget with a force-based stability
check in a learned policy, which is the gap this paper's setup sits in. Recent generators work on
20\,$\times$\,20\,$\times$\,20 grids with eight brick types; our 16\,$\times$\,16\,$\times$\,8 grid and six bricks
are smaller than every setting we found.

\paragraph{Stability and assembly planning.} Our force model is a linear simplification of StableLego
\citep{liu2024stablelego}, which descends from the per-brick equilibrium of \citet{luo2015legolization}. StableLego
caps the friction force at 0.98\,N \emph{per contact point}, places the equilibrium residuals in the objective so
every structure is solvable, and reports 99.6\,\% agreement with 225 physically built samples; it also reports
that a hard-constrained equilibrium formulation was solvable on only 58\,\% of those samples. Our program keeps
hard equilibrium constraints and divides 0.98\,N over the four points of each stud cell, so it is more conservative
on both counts, and an infeasible program is reported as a topple. BrickSim \citep{wen2026bricksim} and BrickFEM
\citep{pletz2023brickfem} are the higher-fidelity alternatives. For assembly order, \citet{liu2025physicsawareasp}
train a PPO planner behind a data-free action mask that includes a per-step StableLego check and reach 100\,\%
physical success where a connectivity-only mask reaches 30 to 93\,\%. Our planner is rule-based and complete for
upright same-height bricks (Appendix~\ref{app:planner}); stability is checked on the final build, with a per-step
option that the benchmark does not use.

\paragraph{Imitation, then reinforcement learning.} Behaviour cloning \citep{pomerleau1989alvinn} and its
compounding-error analysis \citep{ross2011dagger} underlie the SFT stage; learning a policy from a search procedure's
own choices is the AlphaGo recipe \citep{silver2016alphago,silver2017alphagozero,anthony2017exit}. Fine-tuning
with PPO \citep{schulman2017ppo} under a KL penalty towards the initial policy follows \citet{ziegler2019finetuning}
and \citet{ouyang2022instructgpt}; the KL term is what prevents forgetting in VPT \citep{baker2022vpt}, and
PIRLNav \citep{ramrakhya2023pirlnav} reports that the gain from RL fine-tuning shrinks as the imitation data grows,
consistent with our +0.02 to +0.04. \citet{rudner2021klpathologies} describe pathologies of KL-regularised RL with
parametric references. Legal-action masking as a differentiable logit transform is analysed by
\citet{huang2022invalidmasking}; our candidate enumeration makes masking structural rather than a transform on a
fixed action space, as in the object-centric policies of \citet{bapst2019structuredagents} and
\citet{ghasemipour2022blocksassemble}. None of the construction policies above learns a stop action: episodes end
on a budget, a success test or a step cap. The nearest analyses of learned termination come from elsewhere:
option termination collapses without a deliberation cost \citep{harb2017deliberationcost}, navigation agents fail
to stop unless stopping has its own head \citep{xiang2020learningtostop}, and budget-induced terminations should not
be treated as true terminal states \citep{pardo2018timelimits}. We found no prior study of a stop action that is
trained but masked at inference.

\paragraph{Evaluation.} Thingi10K \citep{zhou2016thingi10k} supplies our real meshes; we found no brick-generation
paper that uses it (the field evaluates on ModelNet and ShapeNet derivatives). Occupancy is computed by parity
testing \citep{nooruddin2003voxelization}; volumetric IoU follows the reconstruction literature
\citep{choy2016r2n2,mescheder2019occnet}, whose critics note that IoU rewards coarse agreement
\citep{tatarchenko2019whatdonetworkslearn}. Scoring a failed build as zero is stricter than reporting over valid
structures only, as BrickGPT does. Best-of-$N$ sampling as a test-time-compute baseline follows
\citet{cobbe2021verifiers}, \citet{brown2024monkeys} and \citet{snell2024testtimecompute}. On seeds, every guideline we
know asks for more than two \citep{henderson2018drlmatters,colas2018howmanyseeds,agarwal2021precipice}; we therefore
report ranges and make no significance claims, and follow the reproducibility checklist of \citet{pineau2021reproducibility}
in releasing the records and the audit.
